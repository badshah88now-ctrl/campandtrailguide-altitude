Camping in the Rocky Mountains: High-Altitude Tips

Camping in the Rockies is a different game from a weekend at a lowland state park. Many campgrounds sit above 2,500 metres, where the air is thin, the sun is fierce, and the weather can turn from sunshine to hail in an afternoon. With a little preparation, though, high country is some of the most rewarding camping in the United States.

Give yourself time to adjust

Altitude affects everyone differently. Headaches, poor sleep, and shortness of breath on the first night are common above 2,500 metres. Arrive a day early if you can, drink more water than usual, and keep the first day's hike short and easy. Avoid alcohol on the first evening — it makes altitude symptoms worse and dehydrates you further.

Expect four seasons in one day

Rocky Mountain weather is famously changeable. A July afternoon in Rocky Mountain National Park can start at 25°C and end with a thunderstorm and temperatures near freezing. Pack a waterproof shell and a warm layer even in midsummer, and set up camp with the tent's door facing away from the prevailing wind. Always check the forecast the morning you leave, and be off exposed ridges before afternoon storms build.

Sun, cold, and dry air

At altitude the sun burns faster, so sunscreen, sunglasses, and a hat are essential even on cloudy days. Nights are cold all summer — a sleeping bag rated a few degrees warmer than the forecast low is a wise choice. The dry air also dries out skin and lips, so bring lip balm and moisturiser, and keep drinking water through the evening.

Wildlife and food storage

Black bears roam much of the Rockies, and campgrounds from Colorado to Montana require proper food storage. Use the provided bear boxes where they exist, and never keep food, scented toiletries, or rubbish inside your tent. Store everything at least 100 metres from where you sleep when you are in the backcountry, and cook away from your tent for the same reason.

Plan your route before you go

Popular parks such as Rocky Mountain, Yellowstone, and Grand Teton need reservations months ahead for summer weekends, and many trailheads fill by mid-morning.
